# UX and product acceptance checks

- Trace intended/excluded users, goals, context, accessibility and support constraints to accepted needs; preserve unanswered process and business-rule questions.
- Map the end-to-end service journey, handoffs, waiting, offline/assisted steps, downstream effects, support and recovery beyond the interface.
- Write observable acceptance criteria with actor, state, action, visible result, authoritative state/external effects and failure/recovery; separate quality categories.
- For analytics/optimization/ML/GenAI, verify units, constraints, feasibility, provenance/freshness, uncertainty, invalid output, human override, fallback and escalation where applicable.
- Define research questions and success before sessions; recruit representative actual/likely users, obtain consent, minimize data and use neutral realistic tasks.
- Capture correctness/completion, time or abandonment where useful, errors, assists, confidence and qualitative evidence without generalizing a small sample as population statistics.
- Run UAT on the exact candidate and integrations with independent authorized business acceptors; verify backend state, side effects and test-data cleanup.
- Record accepted/rejected/conditional/not-tested status per scope. Conditional acceptance has owner, conditions and expiry; meetings and demos are not acceptance proof.

ISO [9241-210:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/77520.html) provides current human-centred design principles. The GOV.UK guidance on [simple services](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard/point-4-make-the-service-simple-to-use) and [usability benchmarking](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/measuring-success/usability-benchmarking-a-website-or-whole-service) provides practical public examples; adapt them to the target product and accepted policy.
